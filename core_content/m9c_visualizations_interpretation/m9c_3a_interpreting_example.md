## Example: Interpreting a visualization

1. **Indicator:** Quarterly service volumes for Antenatal Care 1st visit (ANC1) and 4th visit (ANC4)
2. **Level & period:** National level, Jul 2023 to Sep 2025
3. **Comparing:** Quarter-to-quarter changes; green bars = >10% increase, red = >10% decrease, gray = stable
4. **Values:** ANC1 ranges ~49,000–61,000; ANC4 much lower at ~25,000–34,000
5. **Stands out:** ANC1 dropped 13% in Q2 2024 then rebounded 13% by Q4 2024, peaking at ~61,000 in Q1 2025. ANC4 was flat for two years then surged +19% and +14% in early-mid 2025.
6. **So what?** The ANC4 surge in 2025 is the big story — after years of stagnation, are more women completing all four visits? Worth investigating what changed (program, policy, data quality?).

*"Across the country, the number of women attending their first antenatal care visit (ANC1) has gone up and down over time, ranging from about 49,000 to 61,000. There was a noticeable drop of 13% in mid-2024, but numbers bounced back quickly. What's more interesting is what's happening with fourth visits (ANC4) — for almost two years, these stayed flat at around 25,000, but then jumped sharply in early-to-mid 2025 (up 19%, then another 14%). This suggests that more women are completing their recommended visits, not just starting care. It's worth looking into what's behind this change — whether it's due to new programs, policy changes, or improvements in how the data is being recorded."*
