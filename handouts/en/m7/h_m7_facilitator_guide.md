# Facilitator guide — Results Communication

<p class="meta-line"><strong>Facilitator guide</strong> · <strong>Results Communication</strong> · <strong>4 activities + 1 reference · ~95 min</strong></p>

## Purpose

These activities close the workshop. Participants take a FASTR analysis and turn it into something a decision-maker will act on — mapping the audience, writing a sharp finding, linking each finding to a concrete action, and committing to a country action plan for the year ahead. They are mostly **discussion and writing**, not platform work: there is little to demonstrate on screen, and your role is to facilitate clear thinking, not to click through menus.

The single recurring failure across all five handouts is vagueness. By the end, every team should hold a plan in which each line names one specific person, one concrete action, and one real date.

## Session at a glance

| # | Activity | Time | Format |

## Running the session

**Preparation.** These activities work from outputs participants have already produced this week — a chart, a report, a deck. Ask teams to have one of those in front of them. Have the worked example (handout 5) ready to distribute alongside activity 4.

**How to run it.** There is almost nothing to demonstrate on a screen; these are facilitated discussions and writing. Your job is to circulate and push every answer toward the specific. The most useful intervention you make all session is replacing a vague answer with a question: *who, exactly? by when, exactly?*

**Grouping.** Activities 1, 3 and 4 are team activities — seat country teams together. Activity 2 is individual; each participant writes their own finding before comparing.

**Pacing.** End on the action plan (activity 4); it is where the workshop's energy converts into commitment. If time is short, trim activity 1 rather than activity 4. Hand out the worked example *with* activity 4 so teams have a model in view.

**The message to carry through.** Specific beats comprehensive, every time. One named person, one checkable action, one calendar date — anything vaguer will not survive contact with the routine after the workshop.

## The activities

### 1. Map your users · ~20 min · team

**What happens.** A team discussion that maps the audience for a FASTR output before it is communicated: a five-question worksheet completed for *one specific* audience, with a worked example and a share-back.

**Say something like.** *"Not 'the ministry' — one person. And tell me the decision they'll take with this, not just what they're interested in."*

**What a good result looks like.** A worksheet for one named audience whose "decision or action" is a concrete choice that person will make.

**Watch for.**
- Vague answers to "what decision will they take". If a team can't name a concrete decision, they don't yet know the audience — send them to talk to that person.

### 2. Write a finding statement · ~15 min · individual

**What happens.** An individual writing activity that turns a raw finding into a clear statement using the formula *[indicator] [trend] from [value] to [value] in [place/period]. This suggests [interpretation]*, with a fill-in box, worked examples, and a peer check.

**Say something like.** *"The 'this suggests' line is the hard part and the whole point. Don't stop at the numbers — take a position on what they mean."*

**What a good result looks like.** A one- or two-sentence statement a stranger to the data could understand, including an interpretation that takes a stand.

**Watch for.**
- Participants skipping the "this suggests" interpretation — the hardest and most important part. Don't let them stop at the numbers.

### 3. Link your results to actions · ~20 min · team

**What happens.** A worksheet pairing each of the team's top findings with a decision-maker, a concrete action, and a date — a three-row table, with guidance, a worked example, and peer review.

**Say something like.** *"'Share with stakeholders' isn't an action. Name one person and give them one specific ask, by a real date."*

**What a good result looks like.** Three rows, each naming a specific person, a checkable action, and a calendar date.

**Watch for.**
- "Share with stakeholders", "improve data quality", "as soon as possible" — none are real. Push for a named person, a checkable action, and a date.

### 4. Country action plan · ~30 min · team

**What happens.** A team activity that turns "we did a workshop" into an ongoing quarterly rhythm. The worksheet is structured around four dimensions from the post-workshop roadmap — use cases, finalising the report, ongoing updates and dissemination, and skill building — each with a write-in box, followed by a worked example.

**Say something like.** *"Every box needs a name, a cadence, and an existing meeting to plug into. 'Upload data' isn't a plan; 'the data manager uploads by the 5th, presented at the monthly review' is."*

**What a good result looks like.** Four dimensions answered with named owners, real cadences, and existing forums — not verbs without nouns.

**Watch for.**
- Verbs without nouns ("upload data", "share findings"). Push each dimension toward a named person, a real cadence, and an existing forum.
- The *use cases* dimension skipped most often — make them name the questions FASTR will keep answering.

### 5. Worked example — country action plan · ~10 min · reference

**What happens.** A fully filled example plan, built around one finding (low haemoglobin measurement at ANC), used as a model. It fills in the same four dimensions as activity 4: use cases (questions and M&E indicators), report finalisation (what to add, by whom, by when), ongoing updates (responsibilities, forums, calendar), and skill building.

**Say something like.** *"Use this for the level of detail, not the content. Match its specificity — don't copy its answers."*

**What a good result looks like.** Teams using the example as a benchmark for specificity while writing their own plan.

**Watch for.**
- This is a reference, not a worksheet. Hand it out *with* activity 4, and don't let teams copy it — it is there to show the expected level of detail per dimension.

## Closing the session

End on the action plan. The last line of activity 4 is the message to leave participants with: setup is done, the work begins now. The plan is the bridge between the workshop and everyday practice — keep it visible, review it each quarter.
