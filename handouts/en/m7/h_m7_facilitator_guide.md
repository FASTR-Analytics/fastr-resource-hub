# Facilitator guide — Results Communication

<p class="meta-line"><strong>Facilitator guide</strong> · <strong>Results Communication</strong> · <strong>NOT FOR DISTRIBUTION</strong></p>

## About these activities

These activities close the workshop — participants take a FASTR analysis and turn it into something a decision-maker will act on. They are mostly **discussion and writing**, not platform work: there is little to demonstrate on screen, and your job is to facilitate good thinking, not to click through menus.

**Five handouts** — four activities plus one worked-example reference. **~95 min** of participant time.

## How to run it

- Each activity has a **participant handout**; participants work from the handout.
- Activities 1, 3 and 4 are **team activities** — country teams sit together. Activity 2 is **individual**.
- Hand out the **worked example (5)** alongside Activity 4 so teams have a concrete model in front of them — it is a reference, not a worksheet.
- The recurring failure mode across all five activities is **vagueness**. Your main job is to push every answer toward *one person, one concrete action, one real date*.

## The activities

### 1. Map your users

**Activity · ~20 min · team**

**What it is** — a team discussion that maps the audience for a FASTR output before communicating it.
**What the handout covers** — a five-question worksheet completed for *one specific* audience, plus a worked example and a share-back.
**Watch for** — vague answers to "what decision will they take". If a team can't name a concrete decision, they don't know the audience yet — send them to talk to that person.

### 2. Write a finding statement

**Activity · ~15 min · individual**

**What it is** — an individual writing activity that turns a raw finding into a clear, communicable statement.
**What the handout covers** — the *[indicator] [trend] from [value] to [value] in [place/period]. This suggests [interpretation]* formula, a fill-in box, worked examples, and a peer check.
**Watch for** — participants skip the "this suggests" interpretation. It is the hardest and most important part — don't let them stop at the numbers.

### 3. Link your results to actions

**Activity · ~20 min · team**

**What it is** — a worksheet that pairs each finding with a decision-maker, a concrete action, and a date.
**What the handout covers** — a three-row table for the team's top findings, guidance, a worked example, and peer review.
**Watch for** — "share with stakeholders", "improve data quality", "as soon as possible". None are real. Push for a named person, a checkable action, a calendar date.

### 4. Country action plan

**Activity · ~30 min · team**

**What it is** — a team activity that turns "we did a workshop" into an ongoing quarterly rhythm.
**What the handout covers** — a worksheet structured around the four dimensions from the *Post-workshop roadmap* slide: use cases, finalising the report, ongoing updates and dissemination, and skill building. Each dimension has a write-in box; a worked example follows.
**Watch for** — teams answer with verbs but no nouns ("upload data", "share findings"). Push every dimension toward a named person, a real cadence, and an existing forum. The *use cases* dimension is skipped most often — make them name the questions FASTR will keep answering.

### 5. Worked example — country action plan

**Reference · ~10 min**

**What it is** — a fully-filled example plan, built around one finding (low haemoglobin measurement at ANC), used as a model.
**What the handout covers** — the same four dimensions as Activity 4, each filled in: use cases (questions and M&E indicators tracked), report finalisation (what to add, by whom, by when), ongoing updates (responsibilities, forums, calendar), and skill building (training and capacity).
**Watch for** — this is a reference, not a worksheet. Hand it out *with* Activity 4; don't let teams copy it — it's there to show the level of specificity expected for each dimension.

## Wrapping up

End on the action plan. The last line of Activity 4 is the message to leave participants with: *setup is done, the work begins now.* The plan is the bridge between the workshop and everyday practice.
