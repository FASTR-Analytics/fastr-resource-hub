# Facilitator guide — FASTR Quiz

<p class="meta-line"><strong>Facilitator guide</strong> · <strong>FASTR Quiz</strong> · <strong>1 quiz · ~30–45 min</strong></p>

## Purpose

A short, team-based knowledge check on FASTR fundamentals — the framework itself, the data-quality dimensions, and indicator definitions. It works best as an energiser or a recap near the end of the workshop, when participants have enough exposure to debate the answers. Teams may use the AI Assistant to find answers, which doubles as light, low-stakes practice with the tool.

The aim is to surface and settle misconceptions, not to grade. A question that provokes a good argument before the reveal has done its job.

## Session at a glance

| Format | Time | Materials |
|--------|------|-----------|
| Teams, one answer sheet each | ~30–45 min | Participant quiz sheet · AI Assistant (optional) |

## Running the session

**Preparation.** Print one answer sheet per team and decide in advance whether you will run it as a timed race or a relaxed discussion — both work, and the choice sets the energy.

**How to run each question.** Let teams discuss and commit to an answer, then reveal it and give the one-line reason below. Allow teams to debate before you confirm; the disagreement is where the learning happens.

**Using the AI.** Permitting the AI Assistant is deliberate — finding the answer with it is part of the point, and reinforces the habit of checking rather than guessing.

**Tone.** Keep it light. This is a recap, not an examination; the goal is to leave participants clearer, not ranked.

## Answer key

Reveal each answer after teams commit, and give the one-line reason — that explanation is what makes the quiz teach rather than test.

**1. What does FASTR stand for? → B**
*Frequent Assessments and System Tools for Resilience.* Not a "Framework" or "Registry" — FASTR is about rapid, repeated assessment, not a one-off audit. *If a team picks a "Framework" option, ask them what the "rapid-cycle" idea implies — repetition, not a single review.*

**2. The 3 data quality dimensions? → C**
*Outliers, completeness, internal consistency* — the three checks the DQA module runs. Options A and B mix in terms from other quality frameworks; use the reveal to separate FASTR's three from look-alikes.

**3. The 4 technical pillars? → B**
*RMNCAH-N service-use analysis (HMIS), facility phone surveys, household/client phone surveys, rapid qualitative studies.* This is the question teams most often get partly right — they name the HMIS pillar and miss the survey pillars.

**4. 200 ANC1 but 350 ANC4 → A**
*Consistency.* You cannot have more 4th visits than 1st visits — ANC1 must be ≥ ANC4 — so the numbers can't both be right. A useful place to ask *which* check would catch this and why.

**5. ANC1 coverage target population → A**
*Pregnant women* (expected pregnancies) — the denominator for first antenatal visit coverage. If a team picks a different group, link it back to the coverage module: coverage is a count over the population that needed the service.

## Closing the session

Close by naming the one or two questions that produced the most disagreement and restating their answers plainly — those are the misconceptions worth sending participants away without.
