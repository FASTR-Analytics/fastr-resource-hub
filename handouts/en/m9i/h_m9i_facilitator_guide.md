# Facilitator guide — Standard FASTR Reports

<p class="meta-line"><strong>Facilitator guide</strong> · <strong>Standard FASTR Reports</strong> · <strong>1 activity · ~20 min</strong></p>

## Purpose

Standard FASTR Reports are the set of standard prompts that generate FASTR's GFF reports — the disruption report, the regional analysis, and the data-quality appendix. Participants build a report from the prompt library, then work the verification reference below to check it before it leaves the team. The module's value is the discipline it instils: a generated report is a draft until a person has verified it.

By the end, each team should have run a report through both verification passes — the AI's self-check and the team's own — and be able to state that every number is checkable and every interpretation reflects what the team knows.

## Setting up the build (before verification)

Before participants reach the verification activity, they build a report using the prompt library. Three facilitator moves make that build go well:

**Pilot / co-pilot roles.** Choose one person per team as the pilot, who runs the prompts; the rest observe, suggest corrections, and review. Rotate the role if time allows so more than one person practices.

**Define the use case first.** Before opening the prompt library, have each team agree out loud on the use case — country, period, indicator groupings, and who the report is for. Five minutes of agreement here saves thirty minutes of regeneration later.

**Timing reality.** Prompt 1 takes 5–10 minutes to build the deck slide by slide. Tell participants not to click during generation — interrupting can break the flow.

## The activity

### 1. Verifying an AI-generated report · ~20 min · team

**What happens.** A reference and team checklist for verifying a generated disruption report. It introduces the two-pass model — Pass 1 is the AI's self-review via Prompt 5; Pass 2 is the team's own review — and provides section-by-section manual checklists: Section A (general checks), Section B (the base disruption report), and Sections C and D (the optional regional and data-quality appendices).

**Demonstrate.** There is nothing to demonstrate on the platform. Set the activity up by making clear that Pass 1 and Pass 2 are different jobs, and that Pass 2 is the one only the country team can do. Have teams keep the source charts open next to the report as they check.

**Say something like.** *"Prompt 5 checks the deck against itself and the data. It can't know whether a 'disruption' is real or just a clinic that closed for renovation — that's your job, in the second pass."*

**What a good result looks like.** Both passes completed, with the team able to say every number is checkable, every interpretation reflects local knowledge, and no claim is left unsupported.

**Watch for.**
- Teams stopping after the AI pass. Prompt 5 only checks the deck against itself; the manual team review is what catches what only the country team knows — whether a disruption is real or explained by a local event, and whether local context is missing.
- Make sure *both* passes happen — this is the single point of the activity.

## Closing the session

The sign-off line in the handout is the bar: the team must be able to say every number is checkable, every interpretation reflects what they know, and no claim is unsupported. If they cannot, the report is not ready to leave the room.
